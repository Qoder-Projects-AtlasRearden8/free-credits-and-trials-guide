Startup credit programs are not grants. They are a mix of sales, ecosystem development, and product adoption. The largest numbers are usually reserved for companies with venture funding, an accelerator relationship, or a cloud-spend trajectory that makes the credit a sensible acquisition cost.

That does not mean a bootstrapped LLC has no options. It means you should apply for the base tier that actually fits, build something real, and ignore the headline number that requires a funding round you do not have.

> **In plain English:** A $200,000 credit offer is not addressed to a one-person company with a new website. The useful question is not "How much can I get?" It is "What will this program give my company at its current stage, and what will it ask in return?"

## The realistic range for a small LLC

At check time, a bootstrapped, privately held company could reasonably investigate:

- **AWS Activate Founders:** around $1,000 in credits, with a path to up to $5,000 over time according to AWS's own materials.
- **Google for Startups Cloud, pre-funded track:** up to $2,000 in credits.
- **Microsoft for Startups:** a small starting credit—up to $200 at check—with the ability to unlock more as the company verifies and grows.
- **Cloud free trials and always-free allowances:** not startup programs, but often more immediately useful than a large application.
- **Accelerator or investor relationships:** these can unlock larger tiers later, but they usually involve equity, a cohort commitment, or a funding event.

The larger numbers—$100,000 to $350,000—are real offers, but they come with qualification paths. Plan for the base tier first.

## What startup programs are buying

They are not fooled by a pitch deck that uses the word "AI" eleven times. Most programs are evaluating:

- Is this a real, for-profit company with a product and a website?
- Is it at a stage where cloud or model usage will grow?
- Does it have a credible technical team and a real workload?
- Is it eligible in its jurisdiction and funding stage?
- Will the credit turn into a long-term customer relationship, a case study, or a useful ecosystem partner?

For a beginner-education company, the truthful pitch is not "we will become a unicorn." It is:

> We publish practical AI education and small local-first tools for beginners. We have a defined product roadmap, a real audience, and a specific set of workloads we want to test—embeddings, transcription, and small-model inference. We are applying for the base tier so we can build and measure those workloads without pretending to have funding we do not have.

That is a perfectly respectable application. It may not unlock $100,000. It is much more likely to be honest and useful.

## The application kit

Make one folder and keep the documents current and consistent. Most programs ask for a subset of this:

- Legal company name, address, website, and formation date.
- EIN or equivalent tax identifier. Do not put this in a public repository.
- Formation documents or a certificate of good standing, as requested.
- A founder profile and LinkedIn URL.
- A one-page product description: who it helps, what it does, and what is live now.
- A short demo, screenshots, or a public documentation link.
- Funding status: bootstrapped, pre-seed, accelerator, or other. Be precise.
- Current cloud/provider accounts and approximate monthly spend, if any.
- A 6–12 month usage forecast with the workloads that justify the credits.
- A privacy or data-handling statement for any product that touches user data.

Avoid uploading identity documents through a third-party "credit broker." Apply through the provider's own program portal or an authorized accelerator/provider. If someone offers guaranteed approval for a fee, close the tab.

## A 150-word description template

Fill in the brackets. Do not submit the brackets. Do not claim traction you do not have.

> [Company name] is an Indiana-based [education/software/publishing] company formed in [month year]. We help [audience] [specific outcome] through [product or service]. Today, [what exists: a website, a book, a prototype, a waitlist, a small number of customers]. We are applying for [program and tier] to support [specific workloads] for [time period], with an estimated [usage amount] based on [test data or a stated assumption]. We are [bootstrapped / pre-seed / accelerator-affiliated] and are [not currently funded by a partner VC / working with X]. Our next milestone is [measurable goal], and we will track credit consumption monthly against a written budget.

That is enough for many base-tier applications. A longer deck is not a substitute for a real product and a clear use of funds.

## Credit math

A credit is not cash. It is a discount with an expiry date and terms.

- **Credits usually expire.** Put 60-day, 30-day, and 7-day reminders in your calendar. Do not rely on the provider's one "expiring soon" email.
- **Not all services are eligible.** Support plans, marketplace purchases, taxes, and third-party services may be excluded.
- **Usage has to be useful.** Spending more compute just to consume credits is a way to build the wrong product faster.
- **The renewal price is the real price.** If the tool becomes essential after the credit ends, can your business afford the list price?
- **Keep the account separate from production.** A startup credit experiment should not share credentials or billing with your live book shop or client work.
- **Document what the credit bought.** A simple monthly note—usage, outcomes, cost per task—will help with future applications and with pricing decisions.

A good pattern is to set a **credit burn budget**: a monthly usage target that is enough to learn, below the credit balance, and mapped to an experiment with a result. When the experiment ends, stop the resources, even if credits remain. Unspent credits are not a failure; they are a sign you did not manufacture busywork.

## A 30-day plan that respects your time

**Days 1–7:** Build the smallest public asset: a one-page site, a working demo, and a clear description. Set up the free cloud and API accounts you need with budgets.

**Days 8–14:** Define one workload and measure it. For example, 50 short summaries, 20 audio transcriptions, or 100 document embeddings. Record tokens, minutes, and quality.

**Days 15–21:** Apply to the base tier of one or two programs that actually match. Use the application kit and the usage data you just collected. Do not apply to portfolio/VC tiers without the required relationship.

**Days 22–30:** Build the next small feature. If a program rejects you or is closed, note the reason, set a review date, and continue with free tiers or paid usage you can afford. A rejection is not a business verdict.

## If you are in Indiana

Local startup infrastructure can be more valuable than another credit application:

- **[Indiana Small Business Development Center](https://isbdc.org/)** offers advising for Indiana small businesses, often at no cost.
- **[Elevate Ventures](https://elevateventures.com/)** supports Indiana founders and can be a route to accelerators and investor networks.
- **[gener8tor gBETA](https://www.gener8tor.com/gbeta)** runs free accelerator cohorts in several Midwest markets, including Indiana at times; check the current cohort and eligibility.
- University entrepreneurship centers and local chambers can connect you to an AWS Activate Provider or Google for Startups partner. Verify the provider's status on the cloud program's own site.

None of these guarantees credits. They are ways to get advice, a network, and a credible application path. Check current dates and eligibility before you plan around a cohort.

## What not to do

- **Do not claim to be venture-backed to unlock a tier.** Misrepresentation can lead to revocation, repayment, or account closure.
- **Do not apply as a student or nonprofit if you are a for-profit LLC.** Those are separate eligibility categories with separate rules.
- **Do not buy credits from a reseller or marketplace.** It is commonly prohibited and difficult to verify.
- **Do not give a "credit consultant" your provider login.** Use delegated access or the official application portal if a consultant truly needs access.
- **Do not hide a funding round or an accelerator relationship.** Eligibility checks can find public records, and inconsistencies damage future applications.

## Try this now

1. Write your honest company stage in one sentence: "Bootstrapped Indiana LLC, pre-revenue, one founder, building [X]."
2. Pick the one base-tier program that matches that sentence. Put its application deadline and requirements in your tracker.
3. Start a usage log before you apply. One page with date, workload, units, and outcome is enough.
4. If the only available tier requires funding you do not have, write down what milestone would change that and move on. Your time is better spent on the product.
