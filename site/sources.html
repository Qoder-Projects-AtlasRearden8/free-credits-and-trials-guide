Why the date matters

Material changes,
not a museum.

Free programs can close, retire, or change how they count usage. These are the changes worth knowing when an older guide tells you otherwise.

  1. Oct 22, 2026 At check

    Anthropic's public startup cohort was full

    The Claude for Startups page said the current cohort was full and not accepting new applications. Partner offers and VC-route credits were still described, but this was not a self-serve signup bonus on October 22.

    Open the source
  2. Oct 22, 2026 At check

    Together AI no longer offered free trial credits

    Together AI's platform was prepaid at check and required a minimum credit purchase. The old 'free trial credits' advice in many blog posts is stale.

    Open the source
  3. Oct 22, 2026 At check

    AWS Free Tier is a credit-and-plan model for new accounts

    New accounts get $100 in credits, can earn more, and can use a Free account plan for up to six months. The older twelve-month structure is not the right model for a new signup.

    Open the source
  4. Aug 19, 2026 New offer

    Google announced a one-year Gemini student offer

    Eligible U.S. college students could get Google AI Pro for a year; other markets received an AI Plus offer. This is a consumer plan, not Gemini API credits.

    Open the source
  5. Jul 30, 2026 Retirement

    GitHub Models was fully retired

    The playground, model catalog, inference API, and bring-your-own-key features were retired. Free developer tools can disappear even when they are popular.

    Open the source
  6. Jun 25, 2026 Eligibility change

    Cursor closed new student discount sign-ups

    The legacy student discount was discontinued for new applicants; existing students retained access until their plan ended. Many 2025 guides still describe the old offer.

    Open the source
  7. Jun 1, 2026 Pricing change

    GitHub Copilot moved to usage-based billing

    Copilot plans changed how usage and premium requests are counted. The free plan still exists, but the old assumptions about unlimited access do not apply.

    Open the source
