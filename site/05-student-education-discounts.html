Student and education discounts

GitHub, Gemini, Azure, JetBrains, and the changing student discount landscape—plus the honest boundary between learning and running a business.

Student discounts are one of the best values in AI—if you are genuinely a student, using the tools for learning. They are one of the worst foundations for a business—if you are not, or if you use an education license to run commercial work.

This chapter covers the offers that matter at check time, how verification works, and what to do when you are no longer enrolled.

In plain English: Student pricing is a benefit for learning, not a coupon for your company. Use it in the season it belongs to, and build your business on business terms.

How verification usually works

Most student programs ask for one or more of these:

  • A school-issued email address that matches the institution.
  • A student ID card, transcript, or enrollment letter.
  • A GitHub account with two-factor authentication.
  • A verified identity through a third-party service.
  • Proof that you are currently enrolled, not merely an alum.

Verification can take days. Apply before you need the benefit, not the night before a project is due. If you are a distance learner or your school uses an unusual domain, use the support path rather than buying a "verified student" credential from a third party. Those services violate the spirit of the program and sometimes the terms.

What changed in 2026

The student landscape is a good example of why this guide has a date.

  • Cursor closed new sign-ups for its legacy student discount on June 25, 2026. Existing students kept their benefit until the plan's renewal, but new applicants were redirected to general promotions. A blog post from early 2026 would still tell you the opposite. Cursor help page
  • GitHub Copilot moved all plans to usage-based billing on June 1, 2026. The free plan still exists, but the meaning of a "completion" and the availability of premium models changed. GitHub changelog
  • GitHub adjusted its student Copilot plan in March 2026. The current bundle is generous but not identical to older descriptions. Check the plan docs, not a YouTube thumbnail.
  • GitHub Models retired on July 30, 2026. It was a popular student-friendly way to test models; it is gone. Retirement notice

These changes are not reasons to avoid student programs. They are reasons to avoid building a multi-year plan on a discount that can be redesigned in a quarter.

The business boundary

This is the important part for ASDALE DYNAMICS, LLC.

Student benefits are generally intended for the student's education. Some licenses explicitly prohibit commercial use. Others are silent, which is not the same as permission. Using an education email, student verification, or a .edu discount to run paid consulting, a book business, or a SaaS product can violate the provider's terms and, in an education context, your institution's acceptable-use policy.

A clean split looks like this:

ActivityUse
Learning a new IDE, taking a course, a class projectStudent benefit, if you are genuinely enrolled.
Prototyping a personal projectStudent benefit if the license allows it; otherwise a free tier.
Revenue-generating client workBusiness account and commercial plan.
Hosting paid courses, books, or softwareBusiness account, with business billing and a privacy policy.
Publishing an open-source learning projectOpen-source plan or standard free tier; check the license.

If you are not currently a student, do not pretend to be one. The savings are small compared with the risk of losing an account, a code history, or a professional reputation. Use free tiers, open-source tools, and a paid plan for the business.

For teachers and education organizations

Teachers and faculty may qualify for free Copilot access, GitHub Education tools, Google for Education, and Microsoft 365 Education through their institution. These are usually institutional arrangements, not personal discounts for side businesses.

If you build classroom materials:

  • Use student data only in systems the school has approved.
  • Do not upload a student's writing, IEP, health information, or disciplinary record to a free AI tier.
  • Get permission before using student work in a public demo or case study.
  • Follow the school's FERPA, COPPA, and acceptable-use policies.
  • Check the provider's age requirements. Many consumer AI products require users to be at least 13, and some require adult consent for minors.

An AI lesson plan can be excellent. An AI system that collects children's data without institutional approval is a liability, not an innovation.

Scholarship and grant discounts

Some AI tools offer additional discounts through student organizations, hackathons, or university partnerships. These are usually announced through the school or a specific program, not a permanent public page.

If you see a "student discount" advertised by a third party:

  1. Find the same offer on the company's official site.
  2. Check whether the discount requires a paid subscription or a credit card.
  3. Read the renewal terms. A free year can become a full-price annual plan.
  4. Ask what happens to your data when the student status ends.
  5. Do not share your school login with a verification service you do not trust.

If you are no longer a student

You still have good options:

  • Use a free API tier for learning, with synthetic data.
  • Use open-source tools locally (see Chapter 7).
  • Use a free consumer plan for low-stakes tasks.
  • Pay for the one tool that directly supports revenue, instead of trying to recreate a student discount.
  • Check alumni benefits, public-library resources, and community-college continuing-education access. They are often less generous than student offers, but they are legitimate.

The goal is not to extract the maximum discount. It is to keep learning without building your business on an eligibility claim that can disappear.

Try this now

  1. If you are enrolled, visit GitHub Education and check which benefits are still available to you. Add the expiry dates to the tracker.
  2. If you are not enrolled, write "not eligible — use business/free plan" in your notes for student programs. This prevents accidental misuse later.
  3. Find the renewal or end date on any student offer you already use, and set a calendar reminder 14 days before it.
  4. Move any revenue-generating work off an education license and onto a business account.
